ENSC 316 Midterm 1 Prep

The ME1 topic list from the professor’s study guide, matched to Griffiths (4th ed.) and sorted by how likely each topic is to show up. A mock midterm built from the 2017 and 2018 papers follows the map.

Exam format: 1 advanced (35 min), 2 medium (20 min each), 3 beginner (10 min each). 105 minutes total. Bring the printed formula sheet, unmodified except your name and student number.

  • 2.4 energy and 2.5.4 capacitors. Those landed on Midterm 2 and the final instead.

Where Griffiths falls short

The professor parameterizes curves and surfaces with tangent vectors (dTt​, dTα​, …) and builds da and dτ from cross and triple products. Griffiths §1.4 only hands you the standard elements for spherical and cylindrical coordinates. For general parameterized surfaces, use the parametric surfaces section of a multivariable calc book (Stewart has one), or Notaros Ch. 1.

Study order

Seven sessions. Each one leans on the one before it. Read the sections, then try the matching problem before opening the solution.

  1. Coordinates and elements. 1.1.4, 1.4.1, 1.4.2. Memorize dl, da, dτ in all three systems. Drill X7.
  2. Gradient, potential, circulation. 1.2.2–1.2.5, 1.3.3, 2.2.4, 2.3.1–2.3.2. Problems B1 and X6.
  3. Line integrals on strange curves. 1.3.1 plus the parameterization note above. M2, then redo 2018 MT1 #4 without notes.
  4. Flux and the divergence theorem. 1.2.4, 1.3.1 (surface part), 1.3.4. M1 and X2.
  5. Gauss’s law and Coulomb convolution. 2.1, 2.2.1–2.2.3, 2.3.4. B3, X1, X3. Redo 2018 MT1 #5 and #6.
  6. Metals and dipoles. 2.5.1–2.5.3, 3.4.4, 4.1.3. B2 and X4.
  7. Dielectrics. 4.1–4.3, 4.4.1. X5, then A1 on a 35-minute timer. Finish with the whole mock midterm at 105 minutes.

What the grader wants

The 2018 sample papers still have the professor’s marking notes in the margins. Every note says some version of “show the work”:

  • math. A symmetry argument ends in an equation like E=Eγ​(γ)γ^​. A sentence in English isn’t enough.
  • Write out the zeros. In a curl or divergence, show every component that vanishes.
  • Name the identities you use, e.g. ∇⋅(∇×A)=0.
  • One operation per line in Gauss’s-law problems. Draw the Gaussian surface, define every symbol, and label which part is flux and which is enclosed charge.
  • Derive before you quote. C=εS/d only counts if you derived it for that problem.
  • With dielectrics, find D first. Gauss’s law for D only needs free charge. Then E=D/ε.

Mock midterm

Same layout as the real exam. Each problem copies the shape of a 2017/2018 question with new numbers or geometry. Use ε0​=8.854×10−12 F/m and time yourself.

B1 · Electric field from a potential

Beginner · 10 min · like 2017/18 #1

a) V(rf​)=x2yz−2y3+4xz2. Find E at rf​=x^+y^​+2z^.
b) V=γ2sinφ+3z (cylindrical). Find E at γ=2, φ=π/6, z=0.

Solution

(a) E=−∇V with
∇V=(2xyz+4z2)x^+(x2z−6y2)y^​+(x2y+8xz)z^
At (1,1,2): ∇V=20x^−4y^​+17z^, so E=−20x^+4y^​−17z^ V/m​

(b) Watch the 1/γ on the φ term:
∇V=∂γ∂V​γ^​+γ1​∂φ∂V​φ^​+∂z∂V​z^=2γsinφγ^​+γcosφφ^​+3z^
At the point: E=−2γ^​−3​φ^​−3z^ V/m​

B2 · Electrostatic shielding

Beginner · 10 min · like 2017/18 #2

A metal sphere of radius 30 cm carries net charge +2 nC. Inside it is an irregular cavity holding Q1​=+5 nC, Q2​=−1 nC and Q3​=−2 nC at unknown positions. Take V(∞)=0. Find:
a) the total charge on the cavity wall
b) the surface charge density on the outer surface
c) the potential of the metal

Solution

(a) In equilibrium E=0 inside the metal. A Gaussian surface inside the metal that wraps the cavity has zero flux, so Qenc​=0:
qwall​=−(5−1−2) nC=−2 nC
(b) The metal’s charge is conserved: qouter​=+2−(−2)=+4 nC. Cavity charges plus wall charge give zero field in the metal, so the outer surface has no idea where they sit. It spreads uniformly over the sphere:
ρs​=4π(0.3)24×10−9​=3.54 nC/m2
(c) Outside, it looks like a 4 nC point charge at the centre:
V=4πε0​Rqouter​​=0.3(8.99×109)(4×10−9)​≈120 V
Griffiths 2.5.1–2.5.2 (the cavity discussion)

B3 · Two charged sheets

Beginner · 10 min · like 2017 #3

Plane 1 at z=−2 cm has ρsg1​=+8 nC/m². Plane 2 at z=5 cm has ρsg2​=+4 nC/m². Find E in all three regions.

Solution

A single infinite sheet gives ∣E∣=ρs​/(2ε0​), pointing away from positive charge on both sides (Gauss pillbox, 2.2.3). Superpose:
2ε0​ρs1​​=451.8 V/m,2ε0​ρs2​​=225.9 V/m

  • z>5 cm: both point up, E=+677.7z^ V/m
  • −2<z<5 cm: sheet 1 up, sheet 2 down, E=+225.9z^ V/m
  • z<−2 cm: both point down, E=−677.7z^ V/m

M1 · Divergence theorem

Medium · 20 min · like 2018 #3

F(rf​)=4γγ^​+7γzφ^​−3zz^
a) Show the net flux out of a cube of side L doesn’t depend on where the cube sits.
b) Check the divergence theorem directly on a cylinder of radius a, 0≤z≤h, centred on the z-axis.

Solution

(a) Every term written out:
∇⋅F=γ1​∂γ∂​(γ⋅4γ)+γ1​∂φ∂​(7γz)+∂z∂​(−3z)=8+0−3=5
F is continuously differentiable everywhere, so the divergence theorem holds for any closed volume:
∮S​F⋅da=∫V​∇⋅Fdτ=5∫V​dτ=5L3
The divergence is a constant. Only the volume matters, not the position.

(b) Fφ​ is tangent to every face and contributes nothing.

  • Side γ=a, da=adφdzγ^​: ∫0h​∫02π​4a⋅adφdz=8πa2h
  • Top z=h, da=γdγdφz^: (−3h)(πa2)=−3πa2h
  • Bottom z=0, da=−γdγdφz^: Fz​=0 there, so 0

Total 5πa2h=5× volume. It matches.

Griffiths 1.2.4, 1.3.4, 1.4.2

M2 · Line integral on a tilted semicircle

Medium · 20 min · like 2018 #4

Sphere S: x2+y2+z2=A2. Plane P: x−z=0 (the xy-plane rotated 45° about the y-axis). Contour C=S∩P with x>0, from rstart​=Ay^​ to rend​=−Ay^​.

a) Define a parameter u and write the Cartesian coordinates of C.
b) Write dl.
c) Evaluate ∫C​F1​⋅dl for the uniform field F1​=F0​z^.
d) Evaluate ∫C​F2​⋅dl for F2​=AF0​​(yx^−xy^​).
e) Which field is conservative? Prove it, then confirm with a second path for F2​.

Solution

(a) On P, z=x. Put that into S: 2x2+y2=A2. With u∈[0,π]:
x=2​A​sinu,y=Acosu,z=2​A​sinu
Check: u=0 gives (0,A,0), u=π gives (0,−A,0), and x≥0 the whole way.

(b)
dl=dudr​du=A(2​1​cosux^−sinuy^​+2​1​cosuz^)du
(c) F1​⋅dl=2​F0​A​cosudu, and ∫0π​cosudu=0. The answer is 0, which makes sense: F1​=∇(F0​z) and z=0 at both ends.

(d) On C, F2​=F0​(cosux^−2​1​sinuy^​):
F2​⋅dl=F0​A(2​1​cos2u+2​1​sin2u)du=2​F0​A​du ⇒ ∫C​=2​πF0​A​
(e) ∇×F1​=0 since its components are constant. For F2​:
∇×F2​=(∂y​Fz​−∂z​Fy​)x^+(∂z​Fx​−∂x​Fz​)y^​+(∂x​Fy​−∂y​Fx​)z^=0x^+0y^​−A2F0​​z^
Not zero, so F2​ isn’t conservative. Along the straight path on the y-axis (x=0), F2​=AF0​​yx^ and dl=dyy^​. That integral is 0. Two paths, two answers.

Griffiths 1.3.1, 1.3.3, 1.2.5

A1 · Inhomogeneous dielectric sphere

Advanced · 35 min · like 2017 #4

A sphere of radius A sits in vacuum. It’s made of a dielectric with εr​(r)=BrA​ for 0<r<A, B≥1. A free point charge Q is at the centre. Use Gauss’s law and state every symmetry argument.

a) Find D, E, V for r>A.
b) Find D, E, V for 0<r<A.
c) Find P, the bound surface charge density at r=A, and the bound volume charge density.
d) Show the total bound charge is zero.

Solution

Symmetry. The free charge is a point at the origin and ε depends only on r. Rotating about the origin changes nothing, so no component can depend on θ or φ, and no tangential direction is preferred:
D=Dr​(r)r^
Gauss for D on a sphere of radius r. It works in both regions because it only needs free charge:
flux∮D⋅da=Dr​(r)4πr2​​=Qf,enc​Q​​ ⇒ D=4πr2Q​r^for all r
(a) r>A: E=4πε0​r2Q​r^, V=4πε0​rQ​

(b) 0<r<A: ε=ε0​BA/r, so
E=εD​=4πε0​BArQ​r^
V(r)=V(A)+∫rA​Edr′=4πε0​AQ​[1+B1​lnrA​]
(c)
P=D−ε0​E=4πr2Q​(1−BAr​)r^
ρsb​(A)=P⋅r^​r=A​=4πA2Q​(1−B1​),ρvb​=−r21​drd​(r2Pr​)=4πBAr2Q​
(d) Bound volume charge: ∫0A​4πBAr2Q​4πr2dr=Q/B. Bound surface charge: Q(1−1/B). Near r→0, εr​→∞, and the polarization packed around Q carries −∮P⋅da→−Q. Sum: −Q+Q/B+Q−Q/B=0.

Cross-check: net charge inside radius r is Q−Q+Qr/(BA)=Qr/(BA). Dividing by 4πε0​r2 gives back the E from (b).

Griffiths 4.2.1, 4.3.1, 4.4.1 (the metal sphere in a dielectric shell example in 4.4.1 is close)

Extra drills

One per leftover topic, so every box on page 1 of the study guide gets a rep.

X1 · Gauss’s law, infinite charged cylinder

Medium · like 2018 #5

ρvg​=ρ0​(γ/A)2 for 0<γ≤A, zero outside, infinite along z.
a) Argue the coordinate dependence. b) Argue which components survive. c) Find E inside. d) Find E outside.

Solution

(a) Translating in z or rotating in φ leaves the charge unchanged. Components depend on γ only: Ei​=Ei​(γ).

(b) The reflection z→−z leaves the charge alone but would flip Ez​, so Ez​=0. Same with φ→−φ for Eφ​. Result: E=Eγ​(γ)γ^​.

(c) Gaussian cylinder of radius γ, length L. End caps contribute nothing since γ^​⋅z^=0.
Flux: Eγ​2πγL,Charge: ∫0L​∫02π​∫0γ​ρ0​A2γ′2​γ′dγ′dφdz=2A2πρ0​Lγ4​
E=4ε0​A2ρ0​γ3​γ^​(γ<A)
(d) Enclosed charge stops growing at γ=A:
E=4ε0​γρ0​A2​γ^​(γ>A)
Both give ρ0​A/(4ε0​) at γ=A. Continuous, as it should be with no surface charge.

X2 · Total charge on a torus

Medium · like 2017 #6

Same torus as the 2017 exam: central ring radius B, tube radius A, parameters t∈(0,A), α,β∈[0,2π]. This time ρvg​=ρ0​(t/A)2(1+cosα).
Find a) source coordinates, b) tangent vectors, c) dτ, d) total charge.

Solution

(a) γs​=B+tcosα,φs​=β,zs​=tsinα, so rs​=(B+tcosα)γ^​(β)+tsinαz^

(b) Using ∂γ^​/∂φ=φ^​:
dTt​=(cosαγ^​+sinαz^)dt
dTα​=t(−sinαγ^​+cosαz^)dα
dTβ​=(B+tcosα)φ^​dβ
(c) dτ=∣dTt​⋅(dTα​×dTβ​)∣=t(B+tcosα)dtdαdβ

(d) Expand (1+cosα)(B+tcosα) and integrate over α. Only the constant and the cos2α term survive, leaving 2πB+πt:
Q=2πA2ρ0​​∫0A​t3(2πB+πt)dt=π2ρ0​A2(B+52A​)
The real 2017 density had cos(3β) in it. Since ∫02π​cos3βdβ=0, the answer there is 0. That’s the “not as scary as it looks” joke.

X3 · Coulomb convolution, disc and shell

Medium · like 2018 #6

a) A disc of radius R in the xy-plane carries uniform ρs0​. Write the convolution integral for V on the z-axis and evaluate it.
b) Find Ez​ for z>0. Check the limits R→∞ and z≫R.
c) A spherical shell of radius A carries uniform ρs0​. Use the law of cosines to find V on the z-axis inside the shell.

Solution

(a) rs​=γs​γ^​, rf​=zz^, rsf​=γs2​+z2​, das​=γs​dγs​dφs​
V(z)=4πε0​1​∫02π​∫0R​γs2​+z2​ρs0​γs​dγs​dφs​​=2ε0​ρs0​​(z2+R2​−∣z∣)
(b)
Ez​=−dzdV​=2ε0​ρs0​​(1−z2+R2​z​)
As R→∞ this becomes ρs0​/(2ε0​), the infinite sheet. For z≫R, z2+R2​≈z+R2/(2z), so V≈4πε0​zπR2ρs0​​. A point charge.

(c) rsf2​=z2+A2−2zAcosθs​. Substitute w=cosθs​:
∫0π​z2+A2−2zAcosθ​sinθdθ​=zA1​[(z+A)−∣z−A∣]
Inside (z<A) the bracket is 2z, which gives 2/A:
V=4πε0​ρs0​A2⋅2π​⋅A2​=ε0​ρs0​A​
Constant, so no field inside. Outside the bracket is 2A, and you get the 2018 answer ρs0​A2/(ε0​z).

Griffiths 2.1.4, 2.3.4. The spherical shell example there is this exact integral.

X4 · Needle in a dipole field

Medium · like 2017 #5

+Q at (d,0,0), −Q at (−d,0,0). A tiny polarized needle at rf​ with ∥rf​∥≫d rotates freely until it settles. Find the angle C between the needle and the x-axis. Evaluate it at rf​∝(3,0,4).

Solution

The needle acts as a dipole p​n​. Torque N=p​n​×E is zero (and stable) when p​n​∥E. So the needle lines up with E.

Far field of p​=2Qdx^ (Griffiths 3.4.4, coordinate-free form):
E=4πε0​r3p​[3(x^⋅r^)r^−x^]
Let c=x^⋅r^=x/r. Then E⋅x^∝3c2−1 and ∣E∣∝9c2−6c2+1​=1+3c2​:
cosC=1+3c2​3c2−1​=rr2+3x2​3x2−r2​
At (3,0,4) with r=5: cosC=2/(552​)=0.0555, so C≈86.8∘.

For the 2017 version (charges on the z-axis), swap x→z.

X5 · Dielectric boundary

Beginner

Vacuum for z>0, dielectric with εr​=3 for z<0, no free surface charge. Just above the interface, E1​ points into the dielectric at 30° from the normal. Find the angle and magnitude of E2​, and the bound surface charge density.

Solution

Boundary conditions: E1∥​=E2∥​ and D1⊥​=D2⊥​ (no free charge). So
tanθ2​=εr​tanθ1​=3tan30∘=3​ ⇒ θ2​=60∘
E2∥​=E1​/2 and E2⊥​=E1​cos30∘/3=0.289E1​, giving ∣E2​∣=0.577E1​.

P=ε0​(εr​−1)E2​. The dielectric’s outward normal is +z^ and E2z​=−0.289E1​:
ρsb​=P⋅z^=−33​​ε0​E1​≈−0.577ε0​E1​
Griffiths 4.3.3, 4.4.1

X6 · Conservative or not

Beginner

F=(2xy+z)x^+x2y^​+xz^. Is it conservative? If it is, find the work from (0,0,0) to (1,2,3) without doing a line integral.

Solution

∇×F=(0−0)x^+(1−1)y^​+(2x−2x)z^=0
Curl-free everywhere, so it’s conservative. Find f with ∇f=F: f=x2y+xz.
Work =f(1,2,3)−f(0,0,0)=2+3=5. Circulation around any closed loop is 0 (Stokes).

X7 · Coordinate curves and surfaces

Beginner

a) Describe the spherical surface θ=π/4, the cylindrical surface φ=π/3, and the curve γ=2, z=1.
b) Write the outward da on a sphere r=A, a cylinder γ=a, and the cone θ=θ0​.

Solution

(a) θ=π/4 is a cone around the +z-axis with a 45° half-angle. φ=π/3 is a half-plane hinged on the z-axis. γ=2, z=1 is a circle of radius 2 in the plane z=1.

(b)

  • Sphere: A2sinθdθdφr^
  • Cylinder: adφdzγ^​
  • Cone: rsinθ0​drdφθ^ (same geometry as the 2018 Midterm 2 capacitor)
